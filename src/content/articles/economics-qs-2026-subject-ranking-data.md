The highest-scoring programs in Economics & Econometrics are separated by small margins. The top institution, Harvard University, holds the number 1 rank. The scores among the leaders run from the high 90s down through the 80s. Among the top 10, all have scores of 90 or above. The 80–89 band contains 20 institutions, which means that the gap between the very top and the next tier is a matter of a few points rather than a wide chasm.

For readers comparing schools, the practical takeaway is that the top 10 are clustered tightly, and the difference between rank 1 and rank 10 may be smaller than the difference between rank 10 and rank 30. The 70–79 band, with 99 institutions, is where most of the field's well-known research universities sit. The 60–69 band, with 73 programs, includes many solid departments that may be less globally prominent but still meet the ranking's threshold for inclusion.

## What This Means for Subject-Focused Applicants

For a reader choosing a school by subject, several patterns stand out. First, the US and UK dominate the top of the table in absolute numbers, but the field is not limited to those two countries — China, Germany, France, and Italy all have substantial ranked presences. Second, the score distribution shows that the top 10 are tightly grouped, which means that small differences in overall score may not reflect meaningful differences in program quality for a given applicant. Third, the 400 programs outside the top 300 in cumulative terms indicate that a ranked economics program exists in many parts of the world, and that the ranking's coverage is not confined to a small elite set.

The institution at the median position has only a published rank range (351-400), so a median rank cannot be computed. For students who are not aiming at the top 50, there are still hundreds of ranked departments to consider, and the ranking provides a consistent basis for comparison across them.
