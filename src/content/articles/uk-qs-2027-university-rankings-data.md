The United Kingdom places 93 institutions in the QS World University Rankings 2027, against a global list of 1,504 universities across 106 countries and territories. That is 6.2% of the entire ranking. The country's best result is 2nd, held by Imperial College London.

Those headline numbers are easy to quote. The harder question — and the one that matters if you are comparing the UK against other systems — is how those 93 institutions are distributed, and whether the reputational indicators tell the same story as the rank bands.

## Where the 93 institutions actually sit

The distribution is heavily weighted toward the top and the bottom, with a comparatively thin middle.

Seven UK institutions are ranked inside the top 50. Nine more fall in the 51–100 band, bringing the cumulative top-100 count to 16. The 101–150 band holds 7, and 151–200 holds 5, so 28 UK institutions sit within the top 200.

Beyond that, the numbers thin out before swelling again at the lower end. The 201–300 band contains 5 institutions, 301–400 contains 7, and 401–500 contains 8. That gives 48 institutions inside the top 500 — just over half the UK total. The remaining 45 sit beyond 500.

The lower reaches are where the volume is. Twelve institutions fall in the 501–700 band. A further 16 sit between 701 and 1000, and one is ranked 1001 and beyond. Within that 701–1000 stretch, QS publishes only ranges rather than exact positions for most of them: one institution in the 701–710 range, one in 761–770, one in 791–800, four in 801–850, six in 851–900 and three in 901–950.

Below 1000, the pattern continues with ranges: twelve institutions in the 1001–1200 band and four in 1201–1400.

The median rank across the UK's 93 listed institutions is 485.0. That single figure captures the shape of the list better than any band count: half of the UK's ranked institutions sit below the 485th position globally. A country with seven top-50 universities and a median near 500 is not a uniformly strong system — it is a concentrated one.

## What changed against QS 2026

Comparing editions requires care, because QS publishes only a range for some institutions, and a range cannot be compared with an exact rank.

Of the 93 institutions listed in QS 2027, 60 have an exact rank in both the 2027 and 2026 editions. Among those 60, 27 improved, 28 declined and 5 were unchanged. The remaining 29 cannot be compared at all, because their rank is a range in at least one of the two editions.

That split — 27 up, 28 down — is close to a wash. It does not describe a system in broad ascent or broad decline; it describes churn. And it covers only the 60 institutions with comparable exact ranks, not the full UK presence in either edition.

A separate set of counts looks at where today's listed institutions stood in QS 2026. Seven of them were top 50 in 2026. Seventeen were top 100, 22 were top 150, 28 were top 200, 35 were top 300, 43 were top 400, 46 were top 500, 60 were top 700 and 77 were top 1000. Four of the currently listed institutions had no 2026 rank at all.

These figures describe only institutions that appear in QS 2027, measured against their prior position. They are not the UK's total in QS 2026, and they cannot be used to say the country's overall presence grew or shrank between editions.

## Reputation scores: two indicators, two different shapes

QS publishes academic reputation and employer reputation scores for all 93 UK institutions. The two indicators do not distribute the same way.

On academic reputation, 7 institutions score 90 or higher. The cumulative count reaches 13 at 80 or higher, 16 at 70 or higher, 23 at 60 or higher, 27 at 50 or higher, 29 at 40 or higher, 36 at 30 or higher and 44 at 20 or higher.

Looked at as bands rather than cumulative totals, the academic reputation distribution is top-heavy in an unusual way: 7 institutions in the 90–100 band, 6 in the 80–89 band, then a sharp drop to 3 in the 70–79 band, before rising again to 7 in the 60–69 band. The 40–49 band holds just 2.

Employer reputation behaves differently. Eleven institutions score 90 or higher — four more than on academic reputation at the same threshold. The cumulative count is 14 at 80 or higher, 17 at 70 or higher, 20 at 60 or higher, 28 at 50 or higher, 31 at 40 or higher, 34 at 30 or higher and 46 at 20 or higher.

The band view shows where the difference concentrates. Employer reputation has 11 institutions in the 90–100 band, then 3 each in the 80–89, 70–79 and 60–69 bands, 8 in the 50–59 band, 3 in 40–49, 3 in 30–39 and 12 in the 20–29 band.

The practical reading: at the very top of the scale, UK institutions are more likely to be rated highly by employers than by academics. Eleven score 90 or above on employer reputation against 7 on academic reputation. But the academic reputation measure spreads more evenly through the middle of the scale, with 23 institutions at 60 or higher compared with 20 on employer reputation.

For anyone using these scores to compare institutions, the two indicators are not interchangeable proxies for the same quality. A university's position on one does not predict its position on the other.

## Overall scores cover only part of the list

QS publishes an overall score for 60 of the UK's 93 ranked institutions. The other 33 are listed with a rank or rank band but no overall score. The lowest-ranked UK institution with a published overall score sits at 691.

This is not a UK-specific pattern. Globally, 700 institutions in the QS 2027 ranking have a published overall score, and the lowest-ranked of them sits at 696.

A missing overall score carries no information about quality. It does not mean the institution fell below a threshold, and it does not mean it was removed from the ranking — it is still ranked. Any statement about overall-score distribution can only describe the 60 institutions that have one; the other 33 are simply outside that statistic.

## Data notes

The figures in this article come from the QS World University Rankings 2027, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data reference date of 2026-06-18. The full ranking covers 1,504 institutions across 106 countries and territories. Readers can check the underlying list on the QS World University Rankings page.

The UK counts here are produced by filtering that ranking by country or territory, then tallying institutions by rank band and comparing each institution's QS 2027 position with its QS 2026 position. Where QS publishes a rank as a range — for example 601–610 — the comparison uses the start of the range.

Several boundaries limit what these numbers can support. Institutions whose rank is a range in either edition cannot be compared across editions, which is why only 60 of the 93 enter the improved/declined/unchanged counts. The prior-edition counts describe only institutions listed in QS 2027 and say nothing about institutions that were ranked in 2026 but are absent from the 2027 list, so they cannot be read as the UK's 2026 total.

Reputation score bands cover only institutions whose score for that indicator was published. Overall-score figures cover only the 60 UK institutions with a published overall score; the remaining 33 are ranked but unscored, and no conclusion about their standing can be drawn from the absence of a score. Range-based ranks are excluded from all median, average and movement calculations.
