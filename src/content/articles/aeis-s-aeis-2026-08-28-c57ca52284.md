## Understanding the AEIS and S-AEIS Exams

The Admissions Exercise for International Students (AEIS) and the Supplementary Admissions Exercise for International Students (S-AEIS) are two assessment routes for international students seeking admission to Singapore government primary and secondary schools.

The AEIS is typically conducted around September or October, while the S-AEIS is held around February or March. The choice between them depends on the intake period and the student's readiness. Both exams assess English and mathematics, with the level of difficulty adjusted according to the age or level of the student seeking admission.

## Registration Process

Registration for AEIS and S-AEIS is done online through the official website of the Singapore Ministry of Education (MOE). The registration period is usually announced well in advance, and applicants must provide personal details, educational background, and pass a photo. Fees apply for the application, and these are non-refundable.

It is important to note that the registration process is competitive, and places are limited. The MOE advises applicants to consider their child's age and school level carefully, as the exams are not a guarantee of placement.

## Preparation Resources

To prepare for the exams, international students can access a range of resources. The MOE publishes a list of recommended textbooks and syllabuses for each level. Additionally, there are preparatory courses and study guides available from various providers that focus on the format and content of the exams.

Practice is key. Students should familiarise themselves with the structure of the English and mathematics papers, and work on time management. Many resources are designed in line with the Singapore curriculum, helping students to build the necessary skills for the exams.

## Difficulty and Admission Considerations

Admission through AEIS or S-AEIS is not straightforward. The exams are designed to be rigorous, and the number of vacancies in government schools is limited. The MOE considers the student's performance in the exams, as well as the availability of places, when making offers.

International students should also be aware that admission is not guaranteed, and they may need to consider alternative options, such as private schools or international schools, if they do not obtain a place.

For families seeking support, some education consultancies offer guidance on the AEIS and S-AEIS application process, including preparation strategies and school selection advice. It is advisable to seek help from reliable sources, such as official MOE channels, to ensure accurate information.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between AEIS and S-AEIS?**
AEIS is the main admission exercise held in September or October, while S-AEIS is a supplementary exercise held in February or March. The content and format are similar, but the timing and the school intake differ.

**Who can apply for these exams?**
International students who are not Singapore citizens or permanent residents can apply if they meet the age and educational requirements.

**Are the exams compulsory for all international students?**
Yes, for those seeking admission to a government school, passing the relevant exam is a necessary step, though it does not guarantee placement.

**How can I improve my chances of success?**
Thorough preparation, including familiarity with the syllabus and practice, is key. Some families also work with educational consultants who specialise in Singapore admissions.

**Is there any alternative if my child does not pass?**
Yes, options include enrolling in private or international schools in Singapore or waiting for the next exam cycle as long as the child remains eligible by age.
