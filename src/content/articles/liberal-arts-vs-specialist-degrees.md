The liberal arts model (US): 4-year degree, 30–50% of courses in a chosen major, the remainder in general education (science, humanities, social sciences, writing). Designed to produce broadly educated graduates who specialise gradually.

The specialist model (UK, Australia, continental Europe): 3-year degree, 80–100% of courses in a single subject from day one. Designed to produce deep subject expertise quickly.

Tradeoffs: Liberal arts graduates have more career flexibility — they can enter consulting, finance, tech, or government without a directly relevant major. Specialist graduates enter the workforce 1 year earlier and have deeper domain knowledge. UK/Australian employers expect specialist knowledge from new graduates; US employers expect broad skills and train specialists on the job.

Which model works better? The evidence is mixed. US liberal arts graduates report higher career satisfaction and more career changes. UK/Australian specialist graduates report higher early-career salaries in their field. The choice depends on career clarity: if you know exactly what you want (medicine, engineering, accounting), specialist is efficient. If you're uncertain, liberal arts provides exploration space.
