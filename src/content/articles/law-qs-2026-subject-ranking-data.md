The QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, with a data reference date of 2026-04-03, lists 403 institutions worldwide in the Law subject ranking, spanning 61 countries and regions. Harvard University holds the top position. For readers selecting law schools by subject, this article breaks down where law programs are concentrated globally and what separates the leading institutions from the rest of the field.

## Global Concentration: Which Countries Dominate Law Rankings

The United States leads the Law subject ranking with 62 institutions listed, followed by the United Kingdom with 43. China (Mainland) and Italy each have 20 institutions on the list, while Australia follows closely with 19. Spain contributes 15 institutions, Germany 14, Canada 13, France 12, and Brazil 11.

This distribution reveals a clear geographic hierarchy in legal education. The US and UK together account for more than a quarter of all ranked law programs, reflecting the historical dominance of common-law systems in global legal academia. Beyond the Anglo-American core, continental European countries such as Italy, Spain, Germany, and France maintain substantial representation, while Australia and Canada—both common-law jurisdictions—also feature prominently. Notably, China (Mainland) ties with Italy at 20 institutions, signaling the growing international visibility of Chinese law schools.

## The Upper Tier: How the Top 100 Breaks Down

Within the 403 ranked institutions, the upper echelons are tightly structured. The top 10 positions are occupied by 10 institutions, and the top 50 band contains 51 institutions (including ties). The top 100 includes 101 institutions, again reflecting ties at the boundary. A median rank cannot be computed for the 403 listed law programs: the institution at the median position has only a published rank range (151-200), so no single midpoint rank is available.

The score distribution further clarifies the competitive landscape. Only 7 institutions achieve an overall score of 90 or above. The 80–89 band contains 21 institutions, the 70–79 band holds 64 institutions, and the 60–69 band includes 9 institutions. Notably, the score bands cover only the 101 institutions with a published overall score; the remaining 302 institutions are listed with a rank or rank band but no overall score, and a missing score does not mean they scored below any threshold or were dropped from the ranking.

## What Separates the Top Institutions

The score bands reveal a striking gap between the very top and the rest of the field. With only 7 institutions scoring 90 or above, the highest tier of law schools is exceptionally exclusive. The jump from the 80–89 band (21 institutions) to the 70–79 band (64 institutions) represents the largest concentration of ranked programs, suggesting that most law schools cluster in a middle range where competition for visibility and reputation is most intense.

The fact that the published score bands cover only the 101 institutions with a published overall score is itself informative. It suggests that the QS Law subject ranking, which draws on academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, H-index, and international research network indicators, only surfaces schools that already meet a baseline threshold of scholarly output and professional recognition. Institutions without a published overall score are still ranked in the 403-institution list; a missing score does not mean an institution scored below any threshold or was dropped from the ranking.

## Regional Representation Beyond the Top 10

While the US and UK dominate the overall count, the presence of 61 countries and regions in the ranking indicates that legal education has substantial global reach. The inclusion of Brazil (11 institutions) alongside European and North American counterparts points to the growing internationalization of legal scholarship, particularly in jurisdictions where legal systems are undergoing reform or expansion.

China (Mainland) matching Italy at 20 institutions is a notable development. For prospective students, this means that strong law programs exist well beyond the traditional Anglo-American corridor, even if the very highest scores remain concentrated in a small number of institutions.

## Data Notes

The figures in this article come from the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data reference date of 2026-04-03. The Law subject ranking covers 60 subject-specific rankings in total, incorporating academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, H-index, and international research network scores. The data presented here was parsed by subject block and filtered by country, with ranks taken as floor integers. The ranking includes 403 institutions across 61 countries and regions. A median rank cannot be computed for all listed institutions, because the institution at the median position has only a published rank range (151-200). The score bands reflect overall scores as published, and they cover only the 101 institutions with a published overall score; the remaining 302 institutions are listed with a rank or rank band but no overall score, and a missing score does not mean they scored below any threshold. The country leaderboard counts institutions by country of the ranked institution, and ties at rank boundaries result in totals that may exceed the nominal rank number (for example, 51 institutions in the top 50).
