Student satisfaction surveys consistently reveal a counter-intuitive pattern: elite research universities often score lower on teaching satisfaction than teaching-focused institutions. This is not an indictment of elite universities — it reflects structural incentives.

Research universities reward research output (grants, publications) in hiring, promotion, and prestige. Teaching quality is a secondary incentive. Teaching-focused universities reward teaching. The result: students at teaching-focused universities report higher satisfaction with teaching quality, course organisation, and staff availability.

In Australia (QILT SES), regional universities (UNE, CQU) and smaller universities (Bond, Notre Dame) consistently outscore Go8 universities on student satisfaction. In the UK (NSS), post-1992 universities score higher on teaching than Russell Group universities. In the US, liberal arts colleges (Williams, Amherst, Swarthmore) score higher on undergraduate teaching than research universities (Harvard, Stanford).

Actionable advice: If you care about teaching quality and small classes, look beyond the prestige institutions. A university ranked 200th may provide a better undergraduate teaching experience than a university ranked 20th.
