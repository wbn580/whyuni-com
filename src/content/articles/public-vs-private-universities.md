The public/private distinction is one of the most important structural differences across university systems. It affects cost, admissions, and the nature of education.

**Australia**: 37 public, 4 private, 2 international. Go8 universities are all public. Private universities (Bond, Torrens, Notre Dame) are smaller, more expensive, and generally less research-intensive. For most domestic students, public universities are the default — private universities serve specific niches.

**US**: The public/private divide is the defining feature. Public universities (UC Berkeley, UCLA, Michigan, UVA, UNC) provide excellent education at lower cost for in-state students. Private universities (Harvard, Stanford, MIT, Yale) have higher sticker prices but often more generous financial aid. The US News rankings are dominated by private universities, but the top public universities (UC Berkeley, UCLA) rival them in research and prestige.

**UK**: Almost all universities are public (or "publicly funded independent institutions"). The private sector is tiny (<1% of students): University of Buckingham, Regent's University, and a few others. The public/private distinction is largely irrelevant in the UK context.

**Germany**: Nearly all universities are public and tuition-free or low-cost. Private universities exist but are considered less prestigious — the opposite of the US dynamic.

Key takeaway: "Private is better" is a US-centric assumption. A public degree from Melbourne, Toronto, or University of Tokyo is more prestigious than a private degree from a university no one has heard of. The public/private prestige relationship varies by country.
