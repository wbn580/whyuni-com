Different careers have different university preferences. A framework for matching choice to goal.

**Finance/Consulting**: Target universities with strong on-campus recruitment from top firms. In Australia: Melbourne, Sydney, UNSW. In UK: LSE, Oxford, Cambridge, Warwick, Imperial. These are "target schools" — firms allocate recruitment resources to these campuses.

**Tech/Engineering**: University prestige matters less than skills. Strong CS/engineering programmes at any Group of Eight (Australia) or Russell Group (UK) university will open doors. Co-op/internship programmes (Waterloo, UTS) are more valuable than rankings.

**Law**: University prestige is heavily weighted. The "top tier" law firms in Australia recruit predominantly from Go8 law schools. In the UK, the "magic circle" firms recruit from Oxbridge + top Russell Group. In the US, the T14 law schools dominate BigLaw recruitment.

**Medicine**: Accreditation matters more than prestige — any Australian Medical Council-accredited programme leads to the same internship pathways. However, research-oriented medical careers favour research-intensive universities.

**Public Policy / Government**: The Australian Public Service graduate programme recruits from all universities roughly equally. International organisations (UN, World Bank) favour top-ranked universities.

**Academia**: Your PhD supervisor and research group matter more than your university. A star supervisor at a mid-ranked university is better for an academic career than an average supervisor at a top-ranked university.
