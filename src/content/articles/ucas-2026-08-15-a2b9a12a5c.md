## Understanding UCAS's Five Choices

The UCAS system allows you to apply to up to five university courses in a single application. This gives you room to build a balanced list that reflects your academic level and your ambitions. A sensible approach is to organise your five choices into three broad categories: reach, core, and safety.

## The Role of Predicted Grades

Your predicted grades are a central part of your application. They tell universities the level you are expected to achieve. When you choose where to apply, you should compare each course's typical entry requirements with your predicted grades.

- **Reach choices** ask for grades at or slightly above your predictions.
- **Core choices** match your predictions closely.
- **Safety choices** ask for grades comfortably below what you expect to achieve.

By matching each of your five choices to a realistic grade level, you can give yourself a strong chance of receiving offers while still aiming high.

## Writing a Personal Statement that Supports Your Choices

Your personal statement is the one part of the UCAS application where you can explain why you are a good fit for the courses you have chosen. It should show universities that you have thought carefully about your subject and that you have the motivation and skills needed to succeed.

A well-crafted personal statement can strengthen your application to all five choices, even when the courses come from different universities. It allows you to present a coherent picture of your interests and goals, which can help admissions tutors see why you are a suitable candidate.

## Building a Balanced List

There is no single formula for how many reach, core, or safety choices you should make. Your list should reflect your personal circumstances, including how confident you are in your predicted grades and how flexible you are about location or course style.

A typical approach is to include at least one safety choice, one reach choice, and several core choices in between. This way, you maximise your chances of receiving offers while still giving yourself the opportunity to aim higher.

## Final Thoughts

A balanced UCAS application combines careful research with a clear understanding of your own strengths. By using your predicted grades to guide your choices and your personal statement to showcase your potential, you can build a list that gives you the best possible chance of success.
