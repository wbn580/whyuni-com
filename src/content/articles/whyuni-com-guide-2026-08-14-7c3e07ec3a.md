## Overview of New Zealand’s Eight Universities

New Zealand has eight publicly funded universities, each with its own strengths. This article outlines stand-out programs in agriculture, tourism management, and film production, and compares entry requirements for undergraduate (bachelor’s) and postgraduate (master’s) study.

## Agriculture

**University of Lincoln** is known for its focus on land-based studies. Its bachelor’s degrees in agriculture and horticulture typically require completion of a recognised secondary school qualification with sufficient grades. Master’s programs, such as the Master of Agricultural Science, usually ask for a bachelor’s degree in a relevant field, along with evidence of English language proficiency.

**Massey University** offers strong programs in agriculture and agribusiness. For undergraduate entry, you need a secondary school qualification that meets the university’s academic standards. A master’s degree, like the Master of AgriCommerce, generally requires a relevant undergraduate degree.

**University of Otago** provides agricultural programs through its School of Agriculture. Undergraduate entry requires a secondary school certificate. For a master’s, a bachelor’s degree in a related discipline is typically expected.

## Tourism Management

**University of Waikato** offers a Bachelor of Tourism and Hospitality Management, focusing on sustainable tourism. Entry to this bachelor’s requires a recognised secondary school qualification. The Master of Management (Tourism) typically requires a bachelor’s degree, and work experience may be considered.

**Auckland University of Technology (AUT)** has a strong reputation in tourism and hospitality. Its undergraduate tourism programs require completion of secondary school with sufficient grades. For a master’s, such as the Master of International Tourism Management, applicants generally need a bachelor’s degree.

**Victoria University of Wellington** offers tourism studies within its Business School. Undergraduate entry needs a secondary school certificate. For postgraduate study, a relevant bachelor’s degree is usually required.

## Filmmaking and Creative Media

**University of Auckland** offers a Bachelor of Fine Arts with a major in Film and Media. Entry is based on secondary school qualifications and often a portfolio. The Master of Fine Arts program typically requires a relevant undergraduate degree and a portfolio of work.

**Massey University** provides a Bachelor of Communication with a focus on film and media production. Undergraduate entry is similar. The Master of Creative Writing or Master of Fine Arts may ask for a related degree and a creative submission.

**Otago Polytechnic** (though not one of the eight universities) is not included, but within the universities, **Otago** offers a Bachelor of Film and Media. For master’s, a bachelor’s degree is the usual entry requirement.

## Comparing Entry Requirements: Bachelor’s vs. Master’s

Generally, for bachelor’s programs, you need a secondary school qualification, such as NCEA Level 3 or equivalent, with minimum grades. English proficiency is often tested via IELTS or TOEFL. For master’s programs, a completed bachelor’s degree with satisfactory grades is expected, along with a higher level of English proficiency. Some master’s programs may also require relevant work experience or a portfolio.

## Summary

Each of the eight universities has distinct strengths in these areas. Entry requirements vary by institution and program, but the pattern is clear: bachelor’s study builds on school qualifications, while master’s study builds on undergraduate achievement. Always check the specific requirements for your chosen program.
