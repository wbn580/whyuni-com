Australia places 37 institutions in the QS World University Rankings 2027, a list that covers 1,504 universities across 106 countries and territories as of the data cut-off of 18 June 2026. The country's presence is concentrated at the top: six Australian universities sit inside the global top 50, nine make the top 100, and 13 reach the top 200. The best-placed institution is The University of New South Wales (UNSW Sydney) at rank 19. The median rank across all 37 listed Australian universities is 291.0.

## Shape of the Distribution

The distribution is heavily front-loaded. Of the 37 institutions, 6 are ranked between 1 and 50, 3 between 51 and 100, 2 between 101 and 150, and 2 between 151 and 200. That gives 13 institutions inside the top 200. The middle of the pack is thinner: 6 institutions sit between 201 and 300, 3 between 301 and 400, and 5 between 401 and 500. Beyond 500, the tail continues with 5 institutions between 501 and 700, while the published rank bands show 2 institutions in the 851–900 band and 1 in the 951–1000 band, with no institution in the 701–1000 band as a whole and none at 1001 or beyond.

The cumulative picture is worth noting. Australia has 19 institutions in the top 300, 22 in the top 400, and 27 in the top 500. Ten institutions fall outside the top 500, and only 2 are beyond the top 1000. In other words, more than half of Australia's ranked universities — 19 of 37 — are inside the top 300, but the country also maintains a substantial presence in the 500–1000 range, which is where its newer or more specialised institutions tend to cluster.

## Year-on-Year Movement

Compared with the 2026 edition, 31 of the 37 institutions are directly comparable, because only institutions with an exact rank in both editions can be compared; 5 institutions have a rank that is a range in at least one edition and cannot be compared. Among these, 20 improved their rank, 10 declined, and 1 stayed unchanged. The broad direction of travel is upward, but the movement is not uniform across the distribution.

The top of the table held steady. In 2026, Australia had 6 institutions in the top 50, 9 in the top 100, 11 in the top 150, and 13 in the top 200. In 2027, those same counts hold exactly: 6 in the top 50, 9 in the top 100, 11 in the top 150, and 13 in the top 200. The stability at the very top suggests that the leading Australian universities are not gaining or losing ground relative to their global peers at the upper end.

The changes appear further down. In the top 500, the 2026 edition had 28 Australian institutions; the 2027 edition has 27. In the top 700, the count rose from 31 to 32. In the top 1000, it rose from 34 to 35. Among the 37 institutions listed in 2027, 36 also have a 2026 rank, and 1 does not; this data cannot give the 2026 total. So the net movement is mixed: among the institutions listed in 2027, 28 had a 2026 rank inside the top 500 while 27 are inside the top 500 in 2027, and these counts cover only institutions listed in QS 2027 and their QS 2026 rank; 31 had a 2026 rank inside the top 700 while 32 are inside the top 700 in 2027, again covering only institutions listed in QS 2027 and their QS 2026 rank; and 34 had a 2026 rank inside the top 1000 while 35 are inside the top 1000 in 2027, on the same basis of institutions listed in QS 2027 and their QS 2026 rank. These counts cover only institutions listed in 2027 and their 2026 ranks, so they cannot be read as the 2026 total or as institutions dropping out of the list.

## Indicator Gaps: Academic vs Employer Reputation

The QS 2027 data provides separate scores for academic reputation and employer reputation, both scored for all 37 Australian institutions. Comparing the two distributions reveals a pattern that is relevant for readers who use these rankings to compare institutions across countries.

On academic reputation, 6 institutions score 90 or above, and no institution scores between 80 and 89. One institution scores between 70 and 79, 2 between 60 and 69, 3 between 50 and 59, 4 between 40 and 49, 2 between 30 and 39, and 5 between 20 and 29. The remaining institutions score below 20.

On employer reputation, the picture is different. Four institutions score 90 or above, 2 score between 80 and 89, and none score between 70 and 79. Three score between 60 and 69, 2 between 50 and 59, none between 40 and 49, 6 between 30 and 39, and 2 between 20 and 29.

The key difference is at the high end. Six Australian universities have academic reputation scores of 90 or above, but only four reach that level on employer reputation. The gap is not trivial: it suggests that the strongest Australian universities are more highly regarded within academia than by employers, at least as measured by the QS survey-based indicators. At the lower end, the two distributions are closer, with similar numbers of institutions in the 20–59 range.

## What the Distribution Means for Comparative Readers

For readers using the QS 2027 rankings to compare Australia against other countries, the headline figures are straightforward. Australia contributes 37 institutions to a global list of 1,504, which is roughly 2.5% of the total. Its share of the top 50 — 6 out of 50 — is proportionally much larger than its overall share of the list. The country's strength is concentrated in a small number of well-established research universities, with a secondary tier that extends into the top 300.

The year-on-year data shows that the top tier is not shifting. The counts in the top 50, top 100, top 150, and top 200 bands are identical between the 2026 and 2027 editions. Movement is happening in the 200–500 range and below, where individual institutions are gaining or losing places but the overall shape of the distribution remains similar.

The indicator gap between academic and employer reputation is the most notable structural feature. It is not a uniform gap across all institutions — the distributions overlap substantially in the middle ranges — but the concentration of high academic reputation scores among a larger group of institutions than high employer reputation scores suggests that the two measures capture different realities for Australian higher education.

## Data Notes

The figures in this article are derived from the QS World University Rankings 2027, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data cut-off of 18 June 2026. The full ranking includes 1,504 institutions across 106 countries and territories. Counts were cross-checked against the official QS 2027 dataset as of 28 July 2026, with the number of entries matching.

Analysis was performed by filtering the country field for Australia. Rank bands are calculated using the lower bound of each interval as an integer for comparison. The median rank of 291.0 is calculated across all 37 listed Australian institutions.

The year-on-year comparison is based on 31 institutions that have an exact rank in both the 2026 and 2027 editions; one institution is new to the 2027 list and has no prior-year comparison. The score distributions for academic and employer reputation cover all 37 institutions, as both indicators were scored for every Australian university in the 2027 edition. The rank distribution tables reflect the full list as published; where the current edition includes institutions beyond the top 1000, they are counted in the relevant bands but not individually listed in the top-universities table, which covers the top 200 only.
